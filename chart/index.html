    Current heat-pump readings

    Temperatures, operating states and settings last reported by the heat pump. A “readback” is a value the pump reports; a requested setting is what this controller asked it to use.

    Times show receipt by this app. H66 messages usually omit the sensor’s measurement time. Old or unusable readings are marked unavailable for control.

    Husdata H66 data guide

    Husdata H66 connects the heat pump to this app. This guide explains the data series available to recording and history. A series is one value tracked over time, such as supply temperature; its presence here does not mean a current reading is available.

    Compressor activity, heating destination and auxiliary output help estimate electrical power using configured equipment capacity.

        Paired computers Details & controls

        Connection

        Database mirroring

        Mirroring copies the master’s database to the slave, including changes and deletions. After an outage, the slave catches up and verifies the copied snapshot before showing it. A verified snapshot matches the master at the time it was taken; newer measurements can still be waiting to sync.

        Recover missing history

        Use this when the other computer recorded history while this master was offline. Recovery is manual. The master keeps its existing history wherever the two computers disagree, imports compatible missing entries, and rebuilds the learned model from the combined history.

        1. Check what can be recovered

          Compare a snapshot from the other computer with this master. This check changes no history. Review the missing period, counts and skipped entries below before continuing.

        2. Recover the checked gaps

          Import the gaps from that checked snapshot. Conflicting or unsupported entries are skipped. The current model stays available for home control while the rebuilt model catches up and is published.

        3. Make the slave match the master

          Replace the other computer’s database with a verified copy of this master and resume mirroring. Normally, recover the gaps first. You may instead skip recovery after reviewing the check: all unmatched history, including recoverable gaps, will be discarded. No separate archive is kept.

        Move the master role

        When both computers are available and the slave is ready, hand over deliberately. This master finishes control and transfers a final verified snapshot before the other computer starts. The MQTT address moves with the role; devices reconnect and some measurements may be missed.

        A standby, with manual takeover

        If the other computer is still available, use Hand over to the other computer in its master UI. If it has failed, you can promote this computer yourself using the history available here. It never takes over automatically.

        Take over after a failure

        Before force promotion, stop or isolate the old master so it cannot control equipment or keep the MQTT address. Being unreachable alone does not prove it has stopped. Measurements after this computer’s last snapshot may be missing.
