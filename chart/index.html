    Heating strategy & comfortConfigured limits

    Shift heating to lower-cost times when the expected benefit justifies the change. Your comfort limits constrain the plan; the heat model estimates how the house will respond.

    Savings strategy Unavailable

    The strategy sets how much benefit is needed and how much temperature variation is worth accepting within your limits. It does not change those limits or turn price control off.

    • Gentle Current

      Require a larger benefit and favour smaller temperature changes, even if that gives up some savings.

    • Balanced Current

      Weigh worthwhile savings against temperature changes and the length of the intervention.

    • More savings Current

      Accept smaller benefits and more temperature variation to retain the greatest qualifying savings.

    These are saved configuration choices. Edit the Home savings strategy in configuration, then use Apply reviewed configuration in Data & settings. Schedule & away above sets a return time or an automatic resume time.

    Comfort limits apply to the configured indoor average. Bedroom has half the default weight; Upstairs and Downstairs each have a quarter. Individual room temperatures remain visible, but do not impose separate limits.

    Both limits constrain the occupied indoor-average forecast, including temperature uncertainty and any missing-sensor allowance. Away mode lifts these occupied limits until your return; the plan includes reheating costs and checks the limits again from the return time.

    How decisions are made
    1. Compare complete heating cycles. Forecast normal heating alongside possible reductions, with optional preheating. Use current temperatures, stored heat, weather and all-in electricity prices; include recovery, auxiliary heat and the estimated cost of any heat still owed at the end.
    2. Check comfort and available control. Reject forecasts outside average-temperature limits or supported equipment and duration limits. Recheck the chosen plan before it starts and while it runs. Missing required readings, lost forecast coverage or failed checks prevent or end automatic savings.
    3. Choose a worthwhile change. Reduce the estimated benefit by an uncertainty allowance, then require it to cover the strategy’s starting, discomfort and duration allowances. Prefer the mildest qualifying cycle that retains enough of the best benefit. Otherwise leave normal heating in charge.

    The Home heat model supplies temperature, heat-reserve and equipment-response estimates. Validation determines which actions and durations those estimates can support; it does not itself authorize a heating command.

    Savings estimates cover space heating, including recovery. They compare modelled alternatives; changes in hot-water service prevent a whole-house savings claim.

    Learning trials & forecast limits

    Learning-trial settings are unavailable.

    An enabled learning trial can run before normal savings checks are established, within a separate cost budget and stricter cold and delayed-overheating checks. A trial gathers evidence and may cost money; it is not an accepted savings forecast.

    The forecast and comfort limits use the same configured indoor average. A bounded estimate can bridge one unavailable room, with an extra uncertainty allowance. No new learning trial starts with an estimated room, and inferred readings never train the model or fill measured history.

    Control choices

    Automatic heating uses the following phases when their equipment and prediction checks pass.

    Normal heating
    Release price-based changes and use the heat pump’s saved settings. The heat pump decides when heat is needed.
    Preheat
    Restore normal hot-water and auxiliary settings, then raise the saved ROOM demand setting by up to —, subject to device limits. Commissioned floor overrides may open the configured circuits together. ROOM is a demand setting, not a room-air target.
    Reduced heating
    Request tariff reduction. With native heat-pump control available, also lower hot-water settings and restrict auxiliary heating. Automatic hot-water circulation is held back; a run already in progress finishes before reduction starts. A reduction request does not prove that the compressor stopped.
    Recovery
    Release tariff reduction and restore normal ROOM demand. A timed hold can retain reduced hot-water settings and restrict auxiliary heat; indoor-average protection can release the auxiliary restriction early. Saved settings and normal circulation eligibility return at the hold deadline.

    Configured duration limits are unavailable.

    Manual heating override requests the same equipment actions as the corresponding automatic phase, bypassing savings and forecast selection. Reduced heating also applies the available hot-water and auxiliary restrictions and holds back automatic circulation. In Automatic, Normal and Reduced are reassessed on the next controller update. Pause holds them until you select Automatic or the scheduled resume time. Preheat ends at its fixed deadline, or sooner if you select Normal or Reduced; the controller also restores ROOM at that deadline. Equipment availability, native limits and restoration checks still apply.

    Home heat model

    Collecting observations

    The model estimates how the house warms, cools and stores heat. It combines recorded inputs with learned responses and fixed assumptions.

    Planning uses those estimates with prices and comfort limits to choose a heating cycle. Heating control sends the allowed commands. See Heating strategy & comfort for the decision and its rules.

    Learning outcomes · Estimates & checks

    Results from completed cycles and later observations show how well the estimates hold up. Calculated savings compare a recorded cycle with the modeled alternative fixed before it started; they are estimates, not metered savings. Prediction checks help determine which future plans the model can support.

    Validation & evidence

    Reconstructing the model

    Exactly reproducible: the model at a committed learning point, including its coefficients, estimated building reserve and slab state, comfort references and learning evidence.

    Required: the complete learning journal, its saved configuration and initial seed, the same fireplace and sensor-correction revisions, and matching learning software. Keep a consistent full database backup and the corresponding software version; an observation CSV alone is insufficient. Choosing different corrections deliberately produces a revised model.

    Outside this guarantee: every past control choice, discarded or missing measurements, physical command delivery and actual heating outcomes or savings. Recorded observations and forecasts remain evidence; replay does not recreate unrecorded events or prove what the equipment did.

    Model inputs · Recorded & estimated

    Temperatures, heating activity, weather and control context describe what happened. Some inputs are recorded readings; others estimate heat from equipment reports or manual firewood entries. Learning uses these inputs to fit and check the model. Future forecasts instead supply expected weather and proposed heating actions.

              How freeze protection works

              Temporary minimum. When protection activates, it requests a minimum room target. Normal room regulation uses the higher of this minimum and the selected target. The minimum is removed after recovery; the saved selection remains unchanged.

              Heat/On rescue. When the sender requests rescue, the heat-pump controller selects Heat and turns power On, even if the pump was off or in another operating mode. This enables heating; it does not force the compressor to run continuously. Rescue can be active without raising the room target.

              What triggers protection. Air near either probe reaching the configured margin, a predicted loss of pipe reserve, or uncertain pipe history can trigger rescue in both Normal and Away. The initial minimum is 5–16 °C, depending on the measured conditions, and can rise if cold conditions persist. After startup or lost temperature history, pipe estimates stay unavailable until the model establishes their history. This does not mean the pipes are measured as frozen.

              Recovery. The temporary minimum and rescue remain active until both locations meet the recovery conditions for ten minutes. The sender then removes the minimum, and normal room regulation follows your saved target again. Recovery leaves the pump powered on.

              Missing protection input. Once protection inputs are configured on the heat-pump controller, a missing, stale or invalid feed triggers a separate fallback: Heat, power On and a native 16 °C thermostat. Normal room regulation resumes when valid input returns; your saved selection is preserved.

              Room-sensor fault. A missing, stale or out-of-range regulation input also selects the native 16 °C thermostat. It preserves the power setting unless protection requests Heat/On rescue.

              The fixed safety factor doubles cooling heat transfer and halves warming heat transfer. It is applied once in the thermal calculation; the resulting reserve is not divided by it again.

              Calculation & assumptions

              The model tracks heat stored in the water and copper per metre of pipe, separately for the rear and front locations. Pipe dimensions determine their masses and the surface exposed to air. These are engineering assumptions, not learned coefficients.

              Heat capacity

              C = m_water × 4180 + m_copper × 385

              C is heat capacity in J/(m·K); masses are in kg/m. The fixed specific heats are 4,180 J/(kg·K) for water and 385 for copper. Masses use the configured outer diameter and wall thickness, with densities of 1,000 kg/m³ for water and 8,960 for copper.

              Heat exchange with the surrounding air

              G = h × π × D G_cooling = 2 × G G_warming = G / 2

              h is the configured heat-transfer coefficient in W/(m²·K); D is the outside diameter in metres. G is conductance per metre in W/(m·K). The fixed factor makes the estimate lose heat faster and regain it more slowly.

              Pipe temperature response

              dT_pipe/dt = (G_effective / C) × (T_air − T_pipe)

              For liquid water, the estimated pipe temperature follows the air temperature with a response time C/G_effective, in seconds. The calculation uses elapsed time between fresh readings; a warm reading or a heat-pump command cannot reset the estimate.

              Reserve above the protection margin

              reserve = C × max(T_pipe − margin, 0) / 1000

              Reserve is thermal energy in kJ/m above the configured margin. It is zero at or below the margin and unavailable while the temperature history is uncertain. The two locations keep separate reserves.

              Unknown history and possible ice. Without usable history, the model starts from a conservative −40 °C pipe state with possible ice. Long gaps are counted as exposure to −40 °C air. These are assumptions, not measurements. Thawing must repay the ice’s energy debt before positive reserve returns; latent heat never extends permission to remain below the margin.

              Look-ahead and recovery. Protection uses a conservative ten-minute pipe-temperature forecast, extended by the age of the latest reading. Release requires both air readings and both forecasts to stay more than 1 °C above the margin for ten minutes.

              Model limits. The calculation represents a uniform temperature in a water-filled copper pipe. Insulation, airflow, fittings and other stored liquids can respond differently. The estimates do not establish the temperature of every protected part.

              Warming a cold garage

              Stored items and surfaces warm more slowly than the air. After increasing the target, avoid bringing in wet or snowy vehicles or adding substantial moisture for roughly 24 hours, and longer if contents are still cold. Extra moisture can condense on those cold surfaces.

                          About these measurementsTiming, scope and retention

                          Current activity
                          Caught up describes the runtime writer and the shared storage queue. Background storage is shown separately from saving. Other local tasks can still be active. Neither establishes complete source history or successful device actions.
                          Writer timings
                          Successful attempts include writer admission, transaction building, communication and confirmation. Journal sealing includes validation, hashing and SQL. SQLite COMMIT includes its native synchronization; with separate checkpoint scheduling it excludes the later checkpoint. A direct connection may still checkpoint inside COMMIT, as shown in its policy. None of these timings isolates fsync. Approximate p95 is the duration 95% of samples stayed within.
                          Local task duration
                          Elapsed time includes preparation, queueing, computation, transfer and owned cleanup where applicable. It is not SQLite time or application unresponsiveness. A completed download confirms server transfer, not a file saved on the receiving device.
                          History & coverage
                          Evidence is held in memory for up to seven days. Writer history resets when storage reopens; local task history resets when the application exits. Five-minute summaries exclude the oldest partial interval. Earlier and unmonitored work remains unknown.
