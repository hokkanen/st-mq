Heating strategy & comfortConfigured limits

Shift heating to lower-cost times when the expected benefit justifies the change. Your comfort limits constrain the plan; the heat model estimates how the house will respond.

Savings strategy Unavailable

The strategy sets how much benefit is needed and how much temperature variation is worth accepting within your limits. It does not change those limits or turn price control off.

  • Gentle Current

    Require a larger benefit and favour smaller temperature changes, even if that gives up some savings.

  • Balanced Current

    Weigh worthwhile savings against temperature changes and the length of the intervention.

  • More savings Current

    Accept smaller benefits and more temperature variation to retain the greatest qualifying savings.

These are saved configuration choices. Edit the Home savings strategy in configuration, then use Apply configuration in Data & settings. Away & pause above provides temporary changes.

How decisions are made
  1. Compare complete heating cycles. Forecast normal heating alongside possible reductions, with optional preheating. Use current temperatures, stored heat, weather and all-in electricity prices; include recovery, auxiliary heat and the estimated cost of any heat still owed at the end.
  2. Check comfort and available control. Reject forecasts outside room limits or supported equipment and duration limits. Recheck the chosen plan before it starts and while it runs. Missing required readings, lost forecast coverage or failed checks prevent or end automatic savings.
  3. Choose a worthwhile change. Reduce the estimated benefit by an uncertainty allowance, then require it to cover the strategy’s starting, discomfort and duration allowances. Prefer the mildest qualifying cycle that retains enough of the best benefit. Otherwise leave normal heating in charge.

The Home heat model supplies temperature, heat-reserve and equipment-response estimates. Validation determines which actions and durations those estimates can support; it does not itself authorize a heating command.

Savings estimates cover space heating, including recovery. They compare modelled alternatives; changes in hot-water service prevent a whole-house savings claim.

Learning trials & forecast limits

Learning-trial settings are unavailable.

An enabled learning trial can run before normal savings checks are established, within a separate cost budget and stricter cold and delayed-overheating checks. A trial gathers evidence and may cost money; it is not an accepted savings forecast.

Room forecasts follow the common house forecast while preserving each room’s current offset and any available short-term trend. The model does not separately predict how heat travels to each room. Forecast allowances and live checks reduce risk; they cannot guarantee exact room temperatures.

Control choices

Automatic heating uses the following phases when their equipment and prediction checks pass.

Normal heating
Release price-based changes and use the heat pump’s saved settings. The heat pump decides when heat is needed.
Preheat
Raise the saved ROOM demand setting by up to —, subject to device limits. Commissioned floor overrides may open the configured circuits together. ROOM is a demand setting, not a room-air target.
Reduced heating
Request tariff reduction. With native heat-pump control available, also lower hot-water settings and restrict auxiliary heating. Automatic hot-water circulation is held back. A reduction request does not prove that the compressor stopped.
Recovery
Release tariff reduction and restore normal ROOM demand. A timed hold can retain reduced hot-water settings and restrict auxiliary heat; cold-room protection can release the auxiliary restriction early. Saved settings and normal circulation eligibility return at the hold deadline.

Configured duration limits are unavailable.

Home heat model

Collecting observations

The model estimates how the house warms, cools and stores heat. It combines recorded inputs with learned responses and fixed assumptions.

Planning uses those estimates with prices and comfort limits to choose a heating cycle. Heating control sends the allowed commands. See Heating strategy & comfort for the decision and its rules.

Learning outcomes · Estimates & checks

Results from completed cycles and later observations show how well the estimates hold up. Calculated savings compare a recorded cycle with the modeled alternative fixed before it started; they are estimates, not metered savings. Prediction checks help determine which future plans the model can support.

Validation & evidence

Reconstructing the model

Exactly reproducible: the model at a committed learning point, including its coefficients, estimated building reserve and slab state, comfort references and learning evidence.

Required: the complete learning journal, its saved configuration and initial seed, the same fireplace and sensor-correction revisions, and matching learning software. Keep a consistent full database backup and the corresponding software version; an observation CSV alone is insufficient. Choosing different corrections deliberately produces a revised model.

Outside this guarantee: every past control choice, discarded or missing measurements, physical command delivery and actual heating outcomes or savings. Recorded observations and forecasts remain evidence; replay does not recreate unrecorded events or prove what the equipment did.

Learning uses these inputs to fit and check the model. Future forecasts instead supply expected weather and proposed heating actions.

          Heating strategy & protectionStrategy unavailable

          Normal heating is the starting point. The controller can pause the pump during a worthwhile price period, then restore its usual heating. Every strategy keeps the same freezing-protection and recovery requirements.

          Savings strategy

          Choose how much opportunity is worth taking. A stronger savings preference accepts smaller estimated benefits and may keep heating off longer.

          • Gentle Current

            Require a larger benefit and favour shorter pauses, leaving more time for normal heating.

          • Balanced Current

            Keep most of the estimated benefit while avoiding extra off time for small gains.

          • More savings Current

            Accept smaller worthwhile opportunities and choose the greatest estimated benefit within the same limits.

          Edit the savings strategy in configuration, then use Apply configuration in Data & settings. Use Pause price control above to suspend automatic savings.

          How the strategy weighs savings
          Protection boundaries
          Approval unknown

          Protection estimates warmth in a water-filled copper reference pipe beside each temperature sensor. Rear and front are checked independently; whichever needs heat first limits the pause. This estimate guides protection of pipes and stored liquids, but does not measure their actual temperatures.

          Warmth available above the protection margin

          Rear · near pipe—Assessment unavailable

          Stored warmth per metre of the reference pipe, not a countdown. Heating resumes while enough reserve remains for the pump to restore useful heat. Select a value for its evidence.

          Reference & recovery details

          These fixed protection assumptions are separate from the learned cooling forecast. Owner approval records acceptance of this reference for the installation; it does not verify the equipment or the temperatures of everything stored inside.

          How decisions are made
          1. Forecast cooling during a pause. Fresh rear and front temperatures, the outdoor forecast and the Garage heat model estimate how the space would cool while heating is off and through the delay before useful heat returns. Less proven forecasts receive larger uncertainty margins.
          2. Keep protection ahead of savings. Only windows that leave enough reserve at both locations through the delay before useful heat returns can qualify. Expected charging warmth never extends the safe window.
          3. Choose a worthwhile pause and keep checking. Compare electricity avoided at current prices with later recovery electricity and an uncertainty allowance. Choose the shortest qualifying window that keeps the strategy’s share of the best benefit. Reassess as readings and forecasts change; a started pause can end early, but its original endpoint cannot move later.

          Flat prices or an insufficient benefit leave normal heating available. There is no fixed maximum off time: protection, forecast coverage and remaining benefit determine the endpoint. Estimated savings depend on the model’s electricity assumptions; they are not measured savings.

          Paired computers Details & controls

          Connection

          Database mirroring

          Mirroring copies the master’s database to the slave, including changes and deletions. After an outage, the slave catches up and verifies the copied snapshot before showing it. A verified snapshot matches the master at the time it was taken; newer measurements can still be waiting to sync.

          Admin access is required to manage paired computers.

          Recover missing history

          Use this when the other computer recorded history while this master was offline. Recovery is manual. The master keeps its existing history wherever the two computers disagree, imports compatible missing entries, and rebuilds the learned model from the combined history.

          1. Check what can be recovered

            Compare a snapshot from the other computer with this master. This check changes no history. Review the missing period, counts and skipped entries below before continuing.

          2. Recover the checked gaps

            Import the gaps from that checked snapshot. Conflicting or unsupported entries are skipped. The current model stays available for home control while the rebuilt model catches up and is published.

          3. Make the slave match the master

            Replace the other computer’s database with a verified copy of this master and resume mirroring. Normally, recover the gaps first. You may instead skip recovery after reviewing the check: all unmatched history, including recoverable gaps, will be discarded. No separate archive is kept.

          Move the master role

          When both computers are available and the slave is ready, hand over deliberately. This master finishes control and transfers a final verified snapshot before the other computer starts. The MQTT address moves with the role; devices reconnect and some measurements may be missed.

          A standby, with manual takeover

          If the other computer is still available, use Hand over to the other computer in its master UI. If it has failed, you can promote this computer yourself using the history available here. It never takes over automatically.

          Take over after a failure

          Before force promotion, stop or isolate the old master so it cannot control equipment or keep the MQTT address. Being unreachable alone does not prove it has stopped. Measurements after this computer’s last snapshot may be missing.
